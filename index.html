Less sorting. More supporting.

Behind the concept

A self-initiated concept for IT support teams. The starting point is a specific routine: deciding who should own an incoming ticket, rather than promising to resolve every issue.

Show moreShow less

The thinking

Automatic assignment has a clear visual sequence: incoming request → assigned teammate. “Less sorting. More supporting.” connects that mechanism to the team’s work without inventing resolution times or numerical savings.

The creative decision

One ticket and one teammate make the mechanism tangible. The light background gives the headline space, and the coral button completes the reading path. Alex and the VPN request are illustrative examples. Assigned does not mean resolved.

Still assigning tickets by hand?

Behind the concept

A second independent concept about ticket assignment. For the same audience, it opens with a question about a familiar manual task: “Still assigning tickets by hand?”

Same idea. A wider frame.

Behind the concept

The landscape version continues the same IT support story. Its brief is to carry the message into a wider frame while keeping the copy and workflow connected.

A wide layout creates a different reading path: headline, process and action need to follow each other clearly. The adaptation is treated as its own composition rather than a cropped square.

Make sense of subscription revenue

Behind the concept

A concept for subscription SaaS founders and finance teams. It starts with a question about MRR: seeing a revenue change is different from understanding what sits behind it.

Show moreShow less

Subscription revenue movements provide the product foundation. A ChartMogul reference informed the principle of explaining a management question with one infographic; its forecasts and numerical claims were not carried over.

A shortcut. A saved reply.

Behind the concept

A concept for support and operations teams using repeated replies. The brief was to explain how a short abbreviation expands into saved text.

Show moreShow less

The thinking

An email context makes the result concrete. A Grammarly reference informed the presentation of text inside a work setting, but its AI rewriting mechanism is not attributed to TextExpander: this is snippet expansion.

The creative decision

The ;hello block, arrow and complete reply form a clear cause-and-effect sequence. In the selected v03 revision, the abbreviation and arrow were moved closer to the reply. The example email is illustrative; the creative does not promise new text generation or automatic sending.

From knowledge to a draft

Behind the concept

A concept for proposal teams and sales engineering. It starts with company knowledge that already exists before work on the next RFP begins.

Show moreShow less

The thinking

Rather than promising an instant tender win, it explains a process: company resources become the foundation for a draft. Responsive and Loopio references helped focus the hierarchy and give the work a concrete object: a response.

The creative decision

Document categories converge into an RFP draft, while “Ready for your review” preserves a human review step. A light canvas and soft peach accents reflect the brand’s character. Categories and status are illustrative; no speed, accuracy or win-rate figures are used.

02 / APPROACH

First, the why. Then, the way it looks.

Start with the product and the person who needs it. Find one clear message, then build the copy, composition and visual story around it.

01

Understand the product

Explore the audience, problem and product capabilities. Separate verified facts from assumptions.

02

Find the message

Choose one central idea. Find the words and visual approach that explain it together.

03

Build the creative

Work through hierarchy, brand character and detail. Check how the idea reads in the intended format.
